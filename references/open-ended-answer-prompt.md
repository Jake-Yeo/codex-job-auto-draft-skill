# Open-ended application answers

Write the answer a hiring team would get from this applicant in a thoughtful conversation. Read the full question, any instructions beside the field, and the relevant part of the posting. If the question has several parts, give each a concrete answer. Start with the reason, result, or idea the employer asked for.

## Choose the evidence

- Read the relevant experience, projects, and saved writing preferences, including `preferences.application_examples` when present. Start with the applicant’s preferred examples and choose the one that best answers this question. Use a specific action, decision, or difficulty the applicant can explain in an interview. Use a measured result when it adds meaning, or describe what somebody could do because of the work.
- Credit the applicant for their contribution and the team for shared work. Keep metrics and claims tied to their sources. For a hypothetical question, present the work as a proposal and state what you would check with the team first.
- Read the written questions together when they are visible. Let each answer add something. For example, use a short motivation answer to explain the pull of the work, an achievement answer to show ownership, and a longer answer to explain a contribution in detail. Reuse an experience when it is the best evidence for both and each answer draws a different point from it.

## Connect to the company’s purpose

- For questions about motivation, fit, or contribution, identify a verified aim of the company or team. Translate it into a concrete change for the people it serves. Think about what becomes easier, more useful, or more reliable for them when the team does its job well.
- Find a genuine connection to that outcome in what the applicant has chosen to build, improve, or learn. Show the connection through one specific experience, observation, or proposed contribution. Let the reader recognize the shared aim through the details. Use the company’s own mission wording when the question asks about it explicitly, and explain what it would mean in practice.

## Write for the question

- **Why this employer, role, or career direction.** Find one verified detail about the work, product, users, or team that gives this applicant a reason to choose it. Explain why the outcome matters to them and name what they would be excited to work on or learn. Use the detail to make the reason credible.
- **What you built, achieved, or would bring.** Open with what the applicant did and what problem it solved. Show a decision or mechanism that makes their contribution clear. Give the outcome, such as what shipped, what improved, or what the user could do. When the question asks about fit, connect the evidence to a responsibility in the posting.
- **A challenge, mistake, or collaboration.** Choose a real incident with a decision or tradeoff. Give enough context to make the difficulty clear, then explain what the applicant did and how it turned out. Include what they learned or changed when the question calls for it.
- **How you used AI.** Use a documented example from work, school, or a project that matches the question and the applicant’s preferences. Explain the task, what the tool helped with, and the applicant’s decisions, verification, or delivered result. Distinguish AI used during development from a feature that uses AI. Name a tool or model when it helps explain what actually happened.
- **What you would own, build, or ship.** Name one bounded problem and the change it would make for the people affected. Explain why the applicant can take it on with one relevant piece of evidence. For a time-boxed question, describe what they would inspect or learn first, the specific deliverable they would aim to ship in that period, and how they would tell whether it helped. Base the proposal on the posting or product and verify assumptions about the team’s needs as part of the proposed work.

## Revise for the reader

- Write in direct first person with plain verbs. Show enthusiasm through the work the applicant is curious about and the choices they have made. Cut generic praise, résumé recaps, repeated descriptions of the product, and phrases that could come from any applicant. Keep technical details when they explain the work or the judgment behind it.
- Check that the first sentence answers the question, each requested part is covered, and every experience claim is supported. For motivation, ask whether swapping in a different employer would leave the answer intact. If so, make the reason specific to this work. For an example, check that the reader can name what the applicant personally did and why it mattered.
- Fit the requested length and format, including any direction the applicant gives for the current draft. Count words for a word limit and characters for a character limit. When space is tight, keep the strongest reason or contribution and the detail that makes it credible. Use the answer text itself in the field.
